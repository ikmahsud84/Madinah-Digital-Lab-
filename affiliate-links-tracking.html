How Affiliate Links and Tracking Actually Work

Affiliate marketing can seem complicated when you first encounter terms such as affiliate links, referral IDs, cookies, attribution and commissions. In reality, the basic process is fairly simple.

This guide explains how affiliate tracking generally works and what happens after someone clicks an affiliate link.

What Is an Affiliate Link?

An affiliate link is a special URL provided to an affiliate by a company or affiliate network. The link contains information that helps identify the affiliate who referred a visitor.

When someone clicks the link and later completes a qualifying action, the company or network can use the tracking information to determine whether the affiliate should receive credit.

How Affiliate Tracking Works

A typical affiliate journey may look like this:

  1. An affiliate joins an affiliate program.
  2. The company or network provides a unique tracking link.
  3. The affiliate shares useful content containing the link.
  4. A visitor clicks the link.
  5. The visitor is directed to the company's website.
  6. The affiliate system records the referral.
  7. The visitor completes a qualifying action, if applicable.
  8. The transaction is matched to the affiliate.
  9. The commission is recorded and may later be approved.

What Is a Referral ID?

A referral ID is an identifier associated with an affiliate. It may appear as part of an affiliate URL or be stored through another tracking mechanism.

The exact format depends on the affiliate program or network. Its purpose is generally to help identify which affiliate referred the visitor.

What Are Tracking Cookies?

Some affiliate programs use cookies or similar technologies to help remember that a visitor arrived through an affiliate's link.

The tracking period can vary between programs. Some programs may track referrals for a limited number of days, while others may use different attribution methods.

Because tracking methods vary, affiliates should always check the specific terms of the program they join.

What Is Attribution?

Attribution refers to how a company decides which marketing source receives credit for a conversion.

For example, if a customer interacts with several marketing channels before purchasing, the company's attribution rules determine which source receives credit.

Different affiliate programs may use different attribution models and rules.

When Is a Commission Earned?

A commission is usually earned when a referred customer completes an action that qualifies under the affiliate program's terms.

Depending on the program, the qualifying action might be a purchase, subscription, registration, lead or another approved conversion.

A commission may initially appear as pending and may need to be reviewed before it is approved for payment.

Why Can a Commission Sometimes Be Rejected?

A referral does not always result in an approved commission. Possible reasons include cancellations, refunds, duplicate transactions, invalid leads, failure to meet program requirements or other conditions specified by the advertiser.

This is why affiliates should read the program's terms and understand what actions qualify for commission.

Affiliate Networks and Direct Programs

Some companies manage their affiliate programs directly, while others use affiliate networks to handle tracking, reporting and payments.

Affiliate networks can provide a central platform where affiliates can find programs, access tracking links and review performance information.

Why Transparent Affiliate Links Matter

Affiliate marketing should be based on trust. If you use affiliate links, clearly disclose that you may receive a commission when someone makes a qualifying purchase or completes another qualifying action.

A clear disclosure helps readers understand your relationship with the companies or services you recommend.

A Simple Example

Imagine that a software company has an affiliate program. You receive a unique affiliate link and publish a useful guide about the software.

A reader clicks your link, visits the company's website and purchases a qualifying subscription. If the referral is successfully tracked and meets the program's requirements, the sale may be attributed to you and a commission may be recorded.

The exact commission amount, tracking period and approval process depend on the individual affiliate program.

Important Things to Check Before Joining a Program

  • Commission structure
  • Qualifying actions
  • Cookie or attribution period
  • Payment methods
  • Minimum payment threshold
  • Refund and cancellation rules
  • Promotional restrictions
  • Affiliate disclosure requirements
  • Program terms and conditions

Final Thoughts

Affiliate tracking is essentially a system for connecting a referral with the affiliate who generated it. Unique links, referral identifiers and other tracking technologies help companies measure these referrals.

Understanding the process makes affiliate marketing easier to approach responsibly. Focus on useful content, recommend products or services that genuinely fit your audience and always follow the rules of each affiliate program.
